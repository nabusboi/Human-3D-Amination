SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CORE CONCEPT (For Project Introduction)
================================================================================

1.1 What is this Project?
--------------------------
The "Human-to-3D Avatar Movement Detector & Talking Head Engine" is a real-time,
AI-powered motion capture and animated character generation system. Operating
entirely on standard consumer hardware via a standard RGB webcam (no specialized
depth sensors, mocap suits, or multi-camera rigs required), the system tracks a
user's body kinematics, 21-keypoint hand articulation, and 52 facial blendshapes.

It drives a stylized 2.5D "Pixar-grade" animated digital avatar with sub-millisecond
kinematic rendering, dynamic talking-jaw mechanics, volumetric lighting, and
telemetry export capabilities.

1.2 The Problem Statement
--------------------------
- Traditional Motion Capture (MoCap) requires expensive multi-camera studios,
  optical markers, or specialized inertial suits costing thousands of dollars.
- Standard consumer webcam trackers are typically fragmented: one tool tracks
  basic 2D pose, another tracks face landmarks, and connecting them requires
  heavy external game engines (Unity/Unreal Engine) with high latency and GPU overhead.
- AI models for full-body tracking, 3D joint forecasting, and facial animation
  are traditionally isolated into separate massive neural networks, causing
  extreme VRAM usage and sluggish multi-model pipeline bottlenecks.

1.3 The DV Solution
--------------------
- An integrated, single-executable Python pipeline running at 30 FPS in 1280x720.
- Multimodal Perception: YOLOv8 for person localization + MediaPipe for 33-pose
  and 42-hand landmarks (21 per hand) + 52 ARKit Blendshapes for facial expression.
- Unified AI Transformer with Switchable QLoRA: A single neural network backbone
  handling 4 heterogeneous tasks (COCO 2D Pose, CMU MoCap 3D, HDTF Talking Head,
  and Synthetic Faces Camera Pose) using Low-Rank Adaptation (<5% trainable parameters).
- Native Vector-Graphic Avatar Renderer: Custom mathematical rendering engine built
  in pure OpenCV/NumPy that generates smooth volumetric limbs, radial lighting,
  articulated fingers, dynamic oral cavities, and contact shadows without external 3D engines.
- Production Telemetry & Recording: One-touch dual-stream MP4 video and frame-by-frame
  JSON kinematic telemetry export with pre-recording readiness validation.

SECTION 2: END-TO-END SYSTEM PIPELINE & DATAFLOW ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

Every single video frame captured by the webcam executes through a synchronous,
high-throughput 10-stage pipeline:

2.1 Visual Pipeline Flowchart
------------------------------

  [ Webcam Input: 1280x720 @ 30 FPS ]
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 1: Optional Mirror & Preprocessing ]
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 2: YOLOv8 Person Detector (Nano) ] ──> Bounding Box & Confidence Gate
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 3: MediaPipe Multimodal Tracking ]
        ├── 33 Body Pose Landmarks (x, y, z, visibility)
        ├── 21 Hand Landmarks per hand (Left & Right fingers)
        └── 52 ARKit Blendshapes (jawOpen, mouthStretch, blink, etc.)
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 4: Exponential Moving Average (EMA) Smoother ]
        └── Formula: S_t = 0.60 * X_t + 0.40 * S_(t-1)  (Jitter Elimination)
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 5: Unified Multi-Task QLoRA Transformer (Inference) ]
        ├── Refined COCO 17 Keypoints
        ├── HDTF Facial Expression Vector (mouth, stretch, blink, roll)
        └── MoCap 3D Joint Predictions
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 6: Kinematic & Expression Engines ]
        ├── FacialExpressionEngine (Blendshape fusion + Geometric fallback)
        └── MoCap3DRig (3D joint angles & depth-sorted bone occlusion)
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 7: Pre-Recording Readiness Gate ]
        └── Validates visibility of 11 facial + 12 critical body landmarks
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 8: 2.5D Pixar-Grade Vector Avatar Renderer ]
        └── Volumetric shading, radial spotlight backdrop, articulated hands
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 9: Dual-View Compositor & Interactive UI ]
        └── Side-by-side Webcam + Avatar preview + Control Banner
                   │
                   ▼
  [ Stage 10: Multi-Format Exporter & Telemetry / WebSocket ]
        ├── Synchronized MP4 Video Writer
        ├── Timestamped JSON Keypoint Telemetry File
        └── Optional FastAPI / WebSocket Streaming Endpoint


2.2 Detailed Breakdown of Pipeline Stages
------------------------------------------
- Stage 1 (Camera Ingestion): Captures at 1280x720 at 30 FPS. Frames are horizontally
  flipped (`cv2.flip(frame, 1)`) so motion mirrors real user intuition.
- Stage 2 (YOLOv8 Detection): Runs `yolov8n.pt` with a 50% confidence threshold.
  Locates the human subject within the frame and isolates the region of interest.
- Stage 3 (Landmark Extraction): MediaPipe Pose and Hand Landmarkers extract 33
  body landmarks and 42 hand joints. Concurrently, MediaPipe FaceLandmarker with
  blendshapes extracts 52 standard ARKit coefficients.
- Stage 4 (Temporal EMA Filter): Raw landmark streams suffer from high-frequency
  sensor noise (shaking). An EMA filter with alpha=0.60 eliminates jitter while
  maintaining zero perceptible phase lag.
- Stage 5 (QLoRA Neural Fusion): If the trained `unified_multitask_qlora.pt` is loaded,
  the frame passes through the Transformer backbone, blending deep learned pose
  and expression priors with MediaPipe's geometric detections (60% QLoRA / 40% MediaPipe).
- Stage 6 (Rig & Biomechanics Calculation): Calculates 3D elbow and knee flexions,
  evaluates bone depth Z-ordering for accurate limb occlusions (e.g. arm in front of torso),
  and calculates head roll tilt.
- Stage 7 (Readiness Validation): If required face/body landmarks fall below 70%
  confidence, recording is safely locked to prevent corrupted or headless captures.
- Stage 8 (Avatar Generation): Pure procedural rendering using layered vector polygons,
  radial gradient spotlights, anti-aliased capsules, facial cavities, and sneakers.
- Stage 9 (Compositing): Produces a dual-panel split screen (Webcam + Bounding Box
  on the left; Pixar Avatar on the right) plus bottom control banner.
- Stage 10 (Session Persistence): When active, writes synchronized video and a JSON
  telemetry document containing full 33-point kinematics and expression values per frame.

SECTION 3: DEEP DIVE: PERCEPTION LAYER (YOLO, MEDIAPIPE, BLENDSHAPES)
================================================================================

3.1 YOLOv8 Person Detection
- Model: Ultralytics YOLOv8 Nano (`yolov8n.pt`).
- Role: Primary presence detection and human localization.
- Execution: Detects Class 0 ("person") above 0.50 confidence.
- Benefit: Prevents spurious landmark hallucination when no human is present in frame.

3.2 MediaPipe Pose & Hand Landmarker
- Body Pose: 33 keypoints in normalized image coordinates (x, y), depth (z),
  and visibility score.
  * Face: 0-10 (nose, eyes, ears, mouth corners)
  * Upper Body: 11-22 (shoulders, elbows, wrists, pinkies, index, thumbs)
  * Core & Lower Body: 23-32 (hips, knees, ankles, heels, foot index)
- Hand Tracking: 21 landmark nodes per hand (42 total) tracking carpal, MCP,
  PIP, DIP, and tip joints for all 5 fingers.
- Smart Extrapolation: When lower body or arms move partially out of frame, the
  system uses kinematic bone-length heuristics to extrapolate realistic joint positions.

3.3 High-Precision Facial Expression Engine (ARKit Blendshapes)
The system employs a dual-tier expression architecture:
- Primary Tier (ARKit 52 Blendshapes):
  Extracts direct muscular activation coefficients via `face_landmarker.task`:
  * `jawOpen`: Calibrated jaw drop ratio with clean zero-cutoff when mouth is closed.
  * `mouthStretchLeft` / `Right` & `mouthSmileLeft` / `Right`: Dynamic lip stretch metric.
  * `eyeBlinkLeft` / `Right`: Independent eyelid closure tracking.
  * `is_talking`: Evaluated as True when `mouth_open_ratio > 0.15`.
- Secondary Tier (Geometric Pose Fallback):
  If the high-resolution face landmarker is occluded, the engine computes normalized
  facial ratios directly from the 33 pose landmarks (eye-to-mouth vectors, facial
  symmetry axis dot products, eye aspect ratios) with `is_talking > 0.25`.

SECTION 4: DEEP DIVE: AI TRANSFORMER BACKBONE & MULTI-TASK QLORA
================================================================================

4.1 The Architecture: UnifiedMultiTaskTransformer
File: `src/models/unified_multitask_transformer.py`

Rather than maintaining 4 separate neural networks, DV introduces a unified
architecture capable of processing 4 distinct motion and vision modalities.

A. Input Encoders (Task-Specific Adapters):
   - Image Encoder (COCO & Synthetic Faces): 2D Convolutional network
     (7x7 Conv -> BatchNorm -> GELU -> MaxPool -> 3x3 Conv -> BatchNorm -> GELU)
     converting [B, 3, 256, 256] into feature tokens of dimension D=256.
   - MoCap Encoder (CMU 3D MoCap): Linear projection layer mapping sequence of
     31 3D joint positions ([B, seq_len, 93]) to token dimension D=256.
   - Landmark Encoder (HDTF Facial): Linear projection layer mapping 11 facial
     coordinate triplets ([B, 11, 3]) to token dimension D=256.

B. Transformer Backbone:
   - 4 Transformer Encoder Layers.
   - 8 Attention Heads per layer.
   - Embedding Dimension: 256; Feedforward Dimension: 1024 (embed_dim * 4).
   - Base weights initialized with Kaiming uniform and FROZEN permanently.

C. Switchable LoRA Feedforward Layers:
   - Built via custom `SwitchableLoRALinear` layers inside each Transformer block.
   - Low-Rank Decomposition: Instead of updating full weight W (d_in x d_out),
     it computes:
         W_effective = W_frozen + (B_task * A_task) * (alpha / r)
     where:
       * Rank r = 16
       * Alpha = 32 (Scaling multiplier = 32 / 16 = 2.0)
       * A_task is initialized via Kaiming uniform
       * B_task is initialized to zero (ensuring LoRA begins as an identity operation)
   - Trainable Parameters: Only ~203,315 parameters out of ~4.3M total parameters
     are trained per task (<5% of network weights).

D. Task-Specific Output Heads:
   - Linear(256 -> 128) -> GELU -> Linear(128 -> Task Output Dim)
   - Tasks and Output Dimensions:
     1. `coco`  : 17 keypoints x 3 (x, y, visibility) = 51 outputs
     2. `mocap` : 31 joints x 3 (x, y, z)             = 93 outputs
     3. `hdtf`  : 4 talking head metrics (mouth, lip, blink, roll)
     4. `faces` : 25 camera matrix parameters (16 rotation/trans + 9 intrinsics)

4.2 Multi-Task Training Protocol (`train_all.py`)
- Sequential Task Execution: Tasks are trained sequentially (`coco` -> `mocap` -> `hdtf` -> `faces`).
- Strict Isolation: During training of task T, only `lora_A[T]`, `lora_B[T]`, and `task_heads[T]`
  have `requires_grad=True`. All other task adapters and the backbone remain frozen.
- Loss Functions:
  * COCO: Custom Wing Loss on face keypoints (0-4) with 1.5x weight + Smooth L1 Loss on body (5-16).
  * MoCap: Mean Squared Error (MSE) on joint displacement vectors.
  * HDTF: Smooth L1 Loss on talking head action coefficients.
  * Faces: MSE on 3D camera pose and intrinsic parameters.
- Optimizer & Schedule: AdamW (lr=1e-4, weight_decay=1e-4) with Cosine Annealing LR decay,
  Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP float16), and gradient clipping (max_norm=1.0).

SECTION 5: DEEP DIVE: 2.5D PIXAR AVATAR GRAPHICS & TALKING HEAD ENGINE
================================================================================

File: `src/animator/avatar_3d_animator.py`

5.1 Why 2.5D Vector Shading instead of 3D Game Engines?
- Eliminates 3D engine startup delays, IPC socket latency, and VRAM thrashing.
- Produces ultra-clean, resolution-independent vector rendering using GPU-accelerated
  OpenCV rasterization at zero marginal asset footprint.

5.2 Visual Elements of the Stylized Avatar
- Vignette Studio Backdrop: Radial spotlight gradient with deep plum / dark slate tones
  centered behind the character's upper torso.
- Contact Ground Shadow: Perspective-projected diffused ellipse under the sneakers
  that dynamically scales based on avatar hip height.
- Volumetric Shaded Limbs: Upper arms, forearms, thighs, and shins are drawn as
  anti-aliased capsules with dual-tone shading (dark base + specular highlight line).
- Depth-Sorted Occlusion: Joints and limbs are sorted along the Z-axis prior to
  rasterization, allowing natural crossing of arms in front of or behind the torso.
- Royal Blue Polo Shirt: Curved chest polygon with collar accents, button placket,
  and pearl button highlights.
- Articulated 21-Keypoint Hands: Full palm polygon with 5 shaded, multi-joint fingers.
  If hands move out of view, the engine falls back to directional palm nodes.
- Head, Hair & Facial Geometry:
  * Cranium with warm cheekbone radial highlights.
  * Volumetric quiff haircut with forehead swoop and sideburns.
  * Sculpted brown beard conforming to chin tilt.
  * Ears with modeled inner concha cavity depth.
- Expressive Pixar Eyes:
  * White sclera base with blue/hazel iris and pupil.
  * White specular glint reflection dots.
  * Dynamic eyelids that smoothly interpolate during eye blinks.
- Dynamic Speaking Jaw & Mouth Cavity:
  * Mouth opens to reveal a dark crimson oral cavity.
  * Upper teeth row appears naturally during speech.
  * Outer lip contour morphs based on `lip_stretch` and `mouth_open_ratio`.
- Sneakers: Cyan & orange sneakers with white rubber outsoles and directional toe caps.

[Key Bullets]:
- Why LoRA? Full fine-tuning of deep Transformers is memory-intensive and causes
  catastrophic forgetting across tasks.
- Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):
  * Decomposes weight updates into two small rank-16 matrices: W = W_0 + (B x A) * (alpha/r).
  * Base Transformer weights are 100% frozen.
- Switchable LoRA Layers (`SwitchableLoRALinear`):
  * Stores dedicated A and B matrices for each of the 4 tasks inside the same layer.
  * Instantly switches task adapters during forward passes with zero overhead.
- Trainable Parameter Efficiency:
  * Only ~203,000 parameters trained per task out of 4.3 Million total (<5%).
  * Enables full training and inference on standard consumer GPUs (e.g. RTX 3070).
[Speaker Notes]:
"To train multiple tasks without interference or excessive VRAM consumption, we utilize
Low-Rank Adaptation, or LoRA. The base weights of our 4-layer Transformer are frozen.
Inside each feedforward layer, our custom Switchable LoRA module holds tiny rank-16 matrix
pairs for each task. Only 4.7% of the parameters are trained per task. This eliminates
catastrophic forgetting and permits lightning-fast task switching."

SLIDE 9: Temporal Motion Smoothing & Anti-Jitter Filtering
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Slide Title]: Motion Smoothing: Eliminating High-Frequency Camera Jitter
[Visual]     : Waveform comparison graph:
               Top: Jittery raw landmark coordinates.
               Bottom: Smooth, natural trajectory after Exponential Moving Average (EMA).
[Key Bullets]:
- The Problem: Raw webcam sensor noise causes virtual characters to vibrate or jitter
  even when the human subject is standing completely still.
- The Solution: Exponential Moving Average (EMA) Temporal Filter.
  * Mathematical Formula:
      Landmark_smoothed(t) = 0.60 * Landmark_raw(t) + 0.40 * Landmark_smoothed(t-1)
- Optimal Balance:
  * 60% weighting on the current frame preserves immediate responsiveness.
  * 40% weighting on the prior frame absorbs micro-jitters and sensor noise.
  * Delivers fluid, broadcast-quality animation with zero perceived latency.
[Speaker Notes]:
"Anyone who has tested raw webcam tracking knows that keypoints constantly tremble due
to lighting changes and sensor noise. To make our Pixar avatar look professional and stable,
we pass all 33 body coordinates through an Exponential Moving Average filter. By blending
60% of the new frame with 40% of the previous frame, we achieve completely stable poses
without introducing sluggish motion lag."

SECTION 8: VIVA & EVALUATION Q&A DEFENSE (Tough Questions & Model Answers)
================================================================================

Review these questions before your presentation. Evaluators and professors
frequently ask these exact questions:

Q1: "Why do you need YOLOv8 if MediaPipe already detects human poses?"
Answer:
"MediaPipe Pose is optimized for landmark regression once a person is assumed to be
present. However, in cluttered backgrounds or when no user is in front of the camera,
MediaPipe can hallucinate landmarks on inanimate objects like chairs or wall posters.
YOLOv8 serves as a robust presence detector and spatial gatekeeper. It verifies that a
human is legitimately in frame with >50% confidence before landmark tracking engages,
preventing false-positive animations and saving compute cycles."

Q2: "What is LoRA, and why did you use it instead of regular fine-tuning?"
Answer:
"Full fine-tuning updates every parameter in a neural network, which requires storing
massive optimizer states (consuming huge VRAM) and often causes 'catastrophic forgetting'—
where learning a new task destroys performance on prior tasks. LoRA freezes the base
weights and injects low-rank trainable decomposition matrices (A and B) into the linear
layers. With a rank of r=16 and alpha=32, we train only 4.7% of the model's parameters
per task. This allows us to train 4 diverse tasks inside the exact same model while keeping
GPU memory usage low enough to run on consumer graphics cards."

Q3: "How does the system achieve 30 FPS without an external 3D engine like Unity or Unreal?"
Answer:
"External 3D engines incur significant overhead: process-to-process socket serialization,
heavy texture pipelines, shader compilation, and multi-gigabyte memory footprints.
Our engine uses native 2.5D vector rendering implemented directly in OpenCV and NumPy.
By utilizing closed-form mathematical equations for capsule limbs, radial lighting gradients,
and Z-depth sorting, we generate smooth, anti-aliased visual geometry in less than 5 milliseconds
per frame, leaving the remaining 28 milliseconds for camera capture and AI inference."

Q4: "How do you handle occlusions when the user's arm moves behind their body?"
Answer:
"Our pipeline incorporates a MoCap 3D Rig module (`mocap_rig.py`). For every joint,
MediaPipe provides an estimated Z-depth coordinate relative to the hip center. Before
rendering, our animator performs depth sorting on all body segments and limbs. If the
wrist and forearm have a lower Z-value (closer to camera) than the torso, the arm is drawn
after the torso; if higher, it is drawn behind. This delivers genuine 2.5D volumetric
layering without a complex polygon mesh."

Q5: "Why did you choose 52 ARKit Blendshapes over simple landmark distance ratios for facial tracking?"
Answer:
"Simple Euclidean distance between mouth landmarks fails under head rotations—if you turn
your head sideways, 2D mouth landmarks foreshorten, causing the system to falsely register
a closed mouth as talking. ARKit blendshapes decouple facial muscle activations from 3D
head rotation, providing true isolated measurements of jaw depression (`jawOpen`), mouth
stretching (`mouthStretch`), and eyelid state. We also retain a geometric pose fallback
for scenarios where the face is temporarily occluded."

Q6: "What datasets did you use to train the multi-task model, and why?"
Answer:
"We selected four complementary datasets representing different aspects of human motion:
1. COCO 2017: 200,000+ labeled images for robust 2D human pose detection.
2. CMU MoCap: Gold-standard optical 3D motion capture for biomechanical joint dynamics.
3. HDTF (High-Definition Talking Face): 2,000+ videos for synchronized lip and facial motion.
4. EG3D Synthetic Faces: AI-generated faces with ground-truth camera matrices for 3D head pose.
This multi-task dataset diversity trains our shared Transformer backbone to understand
human motion across images, 3D coordinates, and temporal expressions."
